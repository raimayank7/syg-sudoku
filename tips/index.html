Sudoku tips: a routine that always finds the next move

Most people who say "I am stuck" are not missing a clever trick. They are missing a routine: a fixed order of looking, so that no easy move is passed over. Here is the routine we suggest, followed by habits that prevent mistakes.

The routine

  1. Scan the digits 1 to 9 through the boxes. For each digit, check each box that lacks it (cross-hatching). Place what you find.
  2. Repeat until a full round finds nothing. Do not skip this. New moves appear after every placement.
  3. Look at the fullest rows, columns and boxes. For a unit with one gap, fill it. With two or three gaps, name the missing digits and test each gap against its other two units.
  4. Write notes. Only now fill in pencil marks, at least for the crowded parts.
  5. Look for singles in the notes, then for pointing pairs and box-line reductions, then for naked and hidden pairs.
  6. After every removal, go back to step 1. Harder techniques exist to bring the easy ones back.

Where to look first

  • The most common digit. If seven 4s are placed, the last two take seconds to find.
  • The most crowded band. Three boxes in a row with many clues give the quickest cross-hatching.
  • Cells at crossings. An empty cell where a nearly full row meets a nearly full column usually has one candidate.

Habits that prevent mistakes

  • Be sure before you write. A digit placed on a feeling is the start of most broken puzzles. Say the reason to yourself: "5 is the only gap for it in this box."
  • Check all three units. Row, column, box, every time.
  • Update notes at once. When a digit goes in, remove it from the notes it rules out before doing anything else.
  • Do not guess. If you are tempted, you have missed something simpler. Use a hint to see what kind of move is available.
  • Take a break. Looking away for two minutes often shows the move you could not see. On our board, Pause stops the clock.

Using hints well

The Hint button on this site works in two steps. The first tap names the technique and the place to look, for example "look for a hidden single in box 7". Try to find it yourself. The second tap explains the move in full and offers to place the digit. Used this way, a hint teaches; used as a fill-in button, it does not. Times set with hints are not counted as your best.

Moving up a level

  • From Easy to Medium: the techniques are the same, but there are fewer clues, so you need more rounds of scanning, and some singles are found along rows and columns, not in boxes.
  • From Medium to Hard: you need notes, and the first candidate-removing techniques. Learn pointing pairs and naked pairs first; they cover most Hard puzzles.
  • From Hard to Expert: learn triples, the X-Wing and the XY-Wing. Expect one or two such steps in a puzzle; the rest is the usual routine.

A few things that do not help

  • Adding up to 45. It is true of every row, but it only helps when one cell is empty, and then you can see the missing digit anyway.
  • Looking at diagonals. Ordinary sudoku has no diagonal rule.
  • Filling a cell "to see what happens". It may take twenty moves to find the clash, and by then you cannot tell which digits were sound.
