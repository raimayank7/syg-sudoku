Sudoku

How to play sudoku: the rules

Sudoku has one rule, and you can learn it in a minute. It uses numbers, but there is no arithmetic in it: the digits are only nine different symbols, and the puzzle would work just as well with nine letters or nine colours. What you need is patience and a habit of checking.

The grid

A sudoku is a square of 81 cells: nine rows across and nine columns down. Thicker lines divide it into nine boxes of three cells by three. Some cells already contain digits when you start. These are the clues (also called givens), and they never change. Your job is to fill every empty cell.

The one rule

Each row, each column and each box must contain the digits 1 to 9, once each. Said the other way: no digit may appear twice in any row, in any column or in any box.

That is all. A row, a column or a box is called a unit (some books say house). The grid has 27 units, and every cell belongs to exactly three of them: its row, its column and its box. The twenty other cells that share a unit with a cell are the cells it "sees"; none of them may hold the same digit.

One answer, found by reasoning

A proper sudoku has exactly one solution, and you can reach it by logic alone. You never have to guess. Every puzzle made on this site is checked by the program for a single solution, and is also solved by the same step-by-step techniques we teach, before it is shown to you.

Your first moves

  1. Find a crowded box. Look for a box, row or column that already has six or seven digits.
  2. Ask what is missing. If a row lacks only 2 and 7, each of its two empty cells is a 2 or a 7.
  3. Use the other two directions. Look at the column and the box of one of those cells. If a 7 already stands in that column, the cell cannot be 7, so it is 2, and the other cell is 7.
  4. Follow one digit round the grid. Take the digit that appears most often among the clues. For each box that lacks it, see which rows and columns are already closed by that digit. If only one cell of the box stays open, the digit goes there. This is called cross-hatching, and it solves most of an easy puzzle.
  5. Keep going round. Every digit you place closes a row, a column and a box for that digit, and so opens new moves elsewhere.

Notes (pencil marks)

When a cell has two or three possible digits and you cannot decide yet, write them small in the cell. On our board, switch Notes on and tap the digits. Notes are not needed for easy puzzles, but harder ones cannot be solved without them. See how to use pencil marks.

What is not allowed, and what is

  • You may not change a clue.
  • The two long diagonals have no rule in ordinary sudoku. A digit may repeat along a diagonal.
  • You do not have to work in any order. Solve whatever you can see.
  • Guessing is allowed, but it is slow and it is never needed. If you guess and it goes wrong, you must undo everything that followed from it.

How to know you are right

When the grid is full, check any row: it should show 1 to 9 with no repeat. On this site the board tells you when the puzzle is solved, and the Check button marks any digit that does not match the solution. A quick self-test for paper puzzles: the digits in every row, column and box add up to 45.
