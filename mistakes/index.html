Common sudoku mistakes and how to recover

Everybody has reached the last ten cells of a sudoku and found two 8s in one column. It feels like the puzzle is ruined. Often it is not. This page lists the mistakes that cause it, how to catch them early, and how to repair a grid.

The seven usual mistakes

  1. Checking two units, not three. You confirm the row and the column, and forget the box, or the other way round. Fix: a fixed order, always row, column, box.
  2. Misreading a handwritten digit. On paper, a hurried 1 looks like a 7 and a 3 like an 8. Write clearly; cross your 7s if that is your habit.
  3. Stale notes. A digit was placed, but its note remained in another cell of the same unit. Later that note "became" a single. Fix: clean the notes immediately after each placement.
  4. A missing note. While writing candidates you left one out. The cell then looks more limited than it is. Fix: write notes box by box from the list of missing digits, not from memory.
  5. A pattern that is not there. A "pair" whose cells hold a third candidate, or a "pointing pair" with a stray candidate elsewhere in the box. Fix: before removing anything, say the condition aloud and check it.
  6. A guess that was forgotten. You tried a digit "for now" and carried on as if it were certain.
  7. Copying the puzzle wrongly. If you copied a puzzle from a newspaper into an app or onto paper, one wrong clue makes it impossible. Our solver tells you at once whether a grid has no solution, one or many.

Catching a mistake early

  • A cell with no possible digit, or a unit where some digit has no possible cell, means an error has already happened. Stop placing digits.
  • On this site, a digit that repeats in a row, column or box turns red at once. That catches rule breaks. It does not catch a digit that breaks no rule yet but is still wrong.
  • For that, tap Check: it marks every digit that differs from the solution. If you like a safety net, tick "Show mistakes as I type".

Recovering on paper

  1. Do not rub out everything. Find the clash, for example two 8s in column 3.
  2. Ask which 8 you are sure of. If one is a clue, the other is wrong. If both are yours, think back to which you placed with a clear reason.
  3. Remove the doubtful digit and everything that depended on it. This is the hard part. Digits placed after it in the same row, column and box, and then digits that depended on those, are all suspect.
  4. Re-check a few safe areas. Pick two full rows and two full boxes away from the trouble and verify them. If they are clean, the damage is probably local.
  5. If the damage has spread, restart from the clues. It is quicker than hunting. A second attempt at a puzzle you half know takes a few minutes.

Recovering on our board

Undo steps back one move at a time, as far as 200 moves, and restores your notes as they were. So the simplest repair is: tap Check, see which digits are red, and undo back to before the first of them, or just erase the red digits and carry on. Erasing is safe here because Check compares with the true solution, so every digit left on the board is right.

When the puzzle itself seems wrong

Puzzles made on this site always have exactly one solution. If a puzzle from a book or newspaper seems impossible, check your copy against the print, clue by clue, then enter it in the solver. Misprints do happen, though far less often than copying slips.

Fewer mistakes with a calmer pace

Speed comes from a steady routine, not from hurrying. Players who time themselves often find that slowing down at each placement, with one extra glance at the three units, gives a faster finish, because they never lose five minutes to a repair.
