Sudoku glossary: the words solvers use

Sudoku guides use a small vocabulary, and different books use different words for the same thing. This glossary gives each term in plain language, with the other names you may meet. Words that have their own guide are linked.

The grid

Cell
One of the 81 small squares. Each holds one digit when the puzzle is done.
Row, column
A line of nine cells across, or down. Rows are numbered 1 to 9 from the top, columns 1 to 9 from the left.
Box
One of the nine three-by-three squares marked by thick lines. Also called a block or region. We number boxes 1 to 9, left to right and top to bottom, so box 5 is the centre.
Unit
Any row, column or box: a group of nine cells that must hold 1 to 9. Also called a house. There are 27 units.
Band, stack
A band is three boxes side by side (rows 1 to 3, 4 to 6 or 7 to 9). A stack is three boxes one above another. Also called a floor and a tower.
R4C7
Short for "row 4, column 7". This is how we, and most guides, name a cell. The Hint button uses these names.
Peers
The 20 cells that share a row, column or box with a cell. A cell "sees" its peers; none of them may hold the same digit.
Given
A digit printed in the puzzle at the start. Also called a clue. Shown in bold dark type on our board.
Proper puzzle
A puzzle with exactly one solution. Every puzzle made here is proper.
Minimal puzzle
A proper puzzle from which no clue can be removed without allowing a second solution.

Candidates and notes

Candidate
A digit that could still go in a cell, because it does not yet appear in the cell's row, column or box and has not been ruled out by a technique.
Pencil marks
Candidates written small in a cell. Also called notes. See pencil marks.
Bivalue cell
A cell with exactly two candidates. Pairs and wings are built from these.
Elimination
Removing a candidate from a cell because logic shows it cannot be the answer there.
Placement
Writing a digit in a cell as its answer.

Techniques

Scanning, cross-hatching
Following one digit along rows and columns to find its only free cell in a box. See cross-hatching.
Single
A forced placement. A naked single is a cell with one candidate (also: sole candidate). A hidden single is a digit with one possible cell in a unit (also: unique candidate, pinned digit).
Last digit, full house
The simplest single: a unit with only one empty cell.
Locked candidates
The family name for pointing pairs (type 1) and box-line reduction (type 2, also called claiming). Also: intersection removal.
Naked set
A naked pair, triple or quad: N cells in a unit holding only N digits between them.
Hidden set
A hidden pair, triple or quad: N digits confined to N cells of a unit.
Fish
The family of single-digit patterns across lines: X-Wing (two lines), Swordfish (three), Jellyfish (four).
Base and cover
In a fish, the base lines are the ones where the digit is confined; the cover lines are the crossing lines from which it is removed.
Wing
A pattern of three bivalue cells, such as the XY-Wing. The middle cell is the pivot (or hinge); the other two are the wings (or pincers).
Chain
A line of reasoning of the form "if this cell is not 4, then that one is 7, then that one is not 7...". Used in puzzles beyond our Expert level.
Bifurcation
A polite word for trial and error: choose a candidate, follow the consequences, and go back if a contradiction appears. Never needed for puzzles on this site.
Backtracking
How a computer solves by search: try a digit, continue, undo on failure. Our solver uses it to count solutions.

Play and scoring

Grade, level
A label for how hard a puzzle is. See how we grade.
Symmetry
The balanced pattern of clues in most printed puzzles. Ours have rotational symmetry: turn the grid upside down and the clues occupy the same cells.
Seed, puzzle number
On this site every puzzle has a number, shown under the board. The same number always produces the same puzzle, which is how a shared link opens the grid you played.
