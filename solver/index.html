Sudoku

Sudoku solver with steps

Enter the clues of any puzzle. The solver checks the grid, counts its solutions and explains how to solve it by logic.

How to use the solver

  1. Tap a cell and tap a digit to enter each clue of your puzzle. After each digit the selection moves to the next cell, so you can type a whole row. On a keyboard, type the digits and use 0 or the space bar for an empty cell.
  2. Or paste all 81 cells in the box below the board, row by row, using 0 or a dot for empty cells, and tap Load.
  3. Tap Solve.

What it tells you

  • Whether the grid is valid. A digit repeated in a row, column or box is marked in red before anything else is done.
  • How many solutions it has. A search program tries every possibility and reports "no solution", "exactly one" or "more than one". A proper sudoku has exactly one. Two or more usually means a clue is missing; none usually means a clue is mistyped.
  • The solution. Shown in the grid in the lighter digits, with your clues in bold.
  • How a person would solve it. A second program solves the puzzle step by step with the eleven techniques on this site and lists every step in order, each with its reason. You can open the list and follow it on your own copy.
  • A grade. From the hardest technique needed, on the same scale as our own puzzles: singles only, pairs and box-and-line work, or triples, wings and fish.

Two solvers, two jobs

The search program (computer people call it backtracking) is fast and always finds the answer if there is one, but it explains nothing: it simply tries digits and goes back when it hits a wall. The step-by-step solver reasons like a person and explains everything, but it knows only the techniques we teach. For most newspaper puzzles the two agree. For a very hard puzzle the step-by-step solver may stop part-way; the page then says how far logic took it and shows the rest from the search.

Good uses for a solver

  • Checking a puzzle you copied. If you are stuck on a puzzle from a book, enter it before blaming yourself. "No solution" or "more than one solution" means the copy has an error.
  • Learning from one step. Open the list of steps, find the first one you had not seen, close the list and carry on by yourself. That teaches far more than reading the whole answer.
  • Checking a finished grid. Enter your completed grid in full. If it is valid, the solver reports one solution and changes nothing.
  • Grading a puzzle. Curious why a puzzle felt hard? The technique list shows what it needed.

Limits

The solver handles the standard 9 by 9 puzzle only: no diagonal, killer or jigsaw rules. It needs at least 17 clues, the proven minimum for a single solution. Everything runs on your device; the grid you enter is not sent anywhere.

If you would sooner be nudged than shown, play on our own board, where the Hint button gives the technique first and the answer only if you ask again.
