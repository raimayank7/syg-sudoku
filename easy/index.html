What an Easy puzzle asks of you

An Easy puzzle on this site starts with about 38 of the 81 cells filled. At every moment several moves are open, and each can be found with the two simplest ideas: a digit that has only one free cell in a box, row or column (a hidden single), and a cell that has only one possible digit (a naked single). You never need pencil marks, though you may use them.

How to solve it

  1. Take the digit that appears most often among the clues.
  2. For each box without that digit, look along the rows and columns that cross the box. Where the digit already stands in a line, that line is closed. If one cell stays open, tap it and tap the digit.
  3. Do this for all nine digits, then go round again: every placement opens new ones.
  4. When a row, column or box has one or two gaps, fill them by naming the missing digits.

This scan has a name, cross-hatching, and a full guide. New to the game altogether? Read the rules first; they take a minute.

Who this level suits

Beginners, children who have outgrown the mini grids, and anyone who wants a relaxed puzzle with a cup of tea. It is also the right level for practising speed: because no hard step blocks the way, your time depends only on how smoothly you scan. Experienced players use Easy grids as a warm-up.

If you get stuck

On an Easy grid, being stuck always means an open move has been overlooked, usually in a box you have not checked for a particular digit. Tap Hint once: it tells you the box or line to look at without giving the answer. If a digit has turned red, it repeats in its row, column or box; erase it and look again.

When to move up

When you can finish three Easy puzzles in a row without a hint and without a red digit, try Medium. It uses the same two ideas with fewer clues.
