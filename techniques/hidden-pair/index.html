Hidden pair

Two digits fit in only the same two cells of a unit, so those cells can hold nothing else. Level: Hard.

A hidden pair is the same fact as a naked pair seen from the other side. In some row, column or box, two digits (say 1 and 8) can each go in only two cells, and they are the same two cells. Those two cells must therefore be the 1 and the 8 in some order. Any other candidates written in the two cells can be rubbed out.

The pair is hidden because the two cells usually carry extra notes, such as 1 3 8 and 1 5 8 9, and the pattern shows only when you count where each digit can go. After the clean-up the two cells become a naked pair.

How to spot it

  1. Choose a unit with four or more empty cells. Hidden pairs live in crowded notes.
  2. For each missing digit, count in how many cells of the unit it appears as a candidate.
  3. Collect the digits that appear in exactly two cells.
  4. If two of those digits appear in the same two cells, you have a hidden pair.
  5. Remove every other candidate from those two cells.

Why it works

The unit needs both digits. Each of them has only those two cells to choose from. Two digits, two cells: the cells are fully booked. Any third digit placed in one of the cells would leave one of the pair digits without a home in the unit.

Another example

In the second position, count for yourself: go through the unit and tick every cell where each of the two digits could go. You should find the same two cells both times.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Counting a digit that appears in three cells. Each of the two digits must be limited to exactly the same two cells in that unit.
  • Removing the pair digits from other cells of the unit. They are not there to remove: by definition the two digits appear nowhere else in the unit. The removals happen inside the two cells.
  • Giving up because it is slow to find. Look digit by digit, not cell by cell. A count of "appears twice" for two digits in one unit is the signal.

When to look for it

Try hidden pairs when a unit has many empty cells and long lists of notes, and naked pairs are nowhere to be seen. There is a neat link between the two: in a unit with N empty cells, a hidden pair is always matched by a naked group among the other N minus 2 cells. In a busy unit, the hidden pair is the smaller and easier one to find.

Practise it
