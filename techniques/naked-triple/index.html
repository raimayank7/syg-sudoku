Naked triple

Three cells in a unit hold only three digits between them, so those digits leave the rest of the unit. Level: Expert.

A naked triple stretches the naked pair to three cells. In one row, column or box, find three cells whose candidates, taken together, are only three digits, for example 1, 5 and 9. Those three cells will take the three digits in some order, so no other cell in the unit can hold 1, 5 or 9.

The surprise is that the three cells do not each need all three digits. The notes 1 5, 5 9 and 1 9 make a naked triple. So do 1 5 9, 1 5 and 5 9. What matters is the union: three cells, three digits in total.

How to spot it

  1. In a unit, list the cells that have two or three candidates. Cells with four or more cannot be part of a triple.
  2. Pick any three of them and write down all the different digits they contain.
  3. If the list has exactly three digits, it is a naked triple.
  4. Remove those three digits from every other cell in the unit.
  5. If the three cells also lie in a second unit (three cells of one row inside one box), clean that unit too.

Why it works

Three cells need three digits. If their notes offer only three digits in all, each of those digits is needed to fill the three cells. A fourth cell that took one of them would leave two digits for three cells. So the three digits are reserved.

Another example

The second triple is of the "incomplete" kind, where at least one cell holds just two of the three digits. This is the form people miss.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Insisting that every cell shows all three digits. Any mix works, so long as the three cells together show exactly three digits and each cell has at least two.
  • Including a cell with a fourth digit. One extra digit breaks the triple.
  • Choosing cells from different units. All three cells must share one row, one column or one box.

When to look for it

Look for naked triples in units with five or more empty cells after pairs have failed. A unit where several cells are down to two candidates each is the usual hunting ground: three bivalue cells that chain around three digits (1 5, 5 9, 9 1) form a triple.
