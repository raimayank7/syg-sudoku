Naked single

A cell where only one digit is still possible. Level: Easy.

A naked single is the simplest idea in sudoku, and the one that every other technique finally leads to. Take one empty cell and ask: which digits are already used in its row, in its column and in its box? If eight of the nine digits are used somewhere among those three, the ninth is the answer for that cell. Nothing else can go there.

It is called "naked" because, once you write the possible digits (the candidates) in the cell as small notes, the answer stands there alone with nothing hiding it. Some books call it a sole candidate or a forced digit. Whatever the name, the test is the same: count what the cell can see.

How to spot it

  1. Pick an empty cell that sits in a crowded part of the grid: a row, column or box with only two or three gaps is the best place to start.
  2. Run along its row and say the digits you meet. Do the same for its column and for its box.
  3. Note which digits from 1 to 9 you did not say. If only one is missing, write it in.
  4. If two or three are missing, write them as small notes and move on. The cell will become a naked single later, when its neighbours fill up.

A worked example

Why it works

Each row, column and box must hold every digit once. A digit that already stands in the same row, column or box as your cell would be repeated if you used it again. Remove all such digits and what is left is the full list of legal choices. When that list has one entry, there is no choice left to make.

Another example

This one is harder to see without notes, because the eight digits are spread over all three directions: a few in the row, a few in the column and the rest in the box. That is typical of naked singles in the middle of a puzzle.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Counting only the row and the column and forgetting the box. Always check all three. The box is the one people skip.
  • Looking for naked singles everywhere at the start of a puzzle. Early on, most cells have four or five candidates. Scanning for hidden singles is faster at that stage; come back to naked singles when the grid is half full.
  • Trusting notes that are out of date. After every digit you place, strike it from the notes in its row, column and box. On this site the board does that for you.

When to look for it

Naked singles appear in bunches near the end of a puzzle: each digit you place removes a candidate from up to twenty other cells, and some of those drop to a single candidate at once. In Easy and Medium puzzles on this site, singles are all you ever need. In harder puzzles the advanced techniques do not place digits by themselves; they remove candidates until a naked or hidden single appears.

Practise it

Play a puzzle at the Easy level, where this technique is all you need, together with its partner single. The first tap on Hint tells you when it is the step to look for.
