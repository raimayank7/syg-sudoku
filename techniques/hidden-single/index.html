Hidden single

A digit that has only one possible cell in a row, column or box. Level: Easy.

A hidden single turns the question around. Instead of asking "what can go in this cell?", ask "where can this digit go in this box?" Every box, row and column needs each digit once. If, for some digit, all the empty cells of a box except one are ruled out, the digit must go in that one cell, even though the cell itself may still seem to allow several digits.

That is why it is "hidden": in pencil marks the cell may show three or four candidates, and the right one does not stand out until you notice that it appears nowhere else in the unit. Hidden singles are the workhorse of sudoku. Most newspaper puzzles marked easy or medium can be finished with nothing else.

How to spot it

  1. Choose a digit that already appears many times in the grid. A digit placed five or six times has few homes left.
  2. Choose a box that does not have that digit yet.
  3. Look at the rows and columns that cross the box. Wherever the digit already stands in one of those lines, that whole line is closed inside your box. This sweep is called cross-hatching.
  4. Count the empty cells of the box that remain open. If there is one, place the digit. If there are two or three, move to the next box or the next digit.
  5. Repeat the same idea along rows and columns: for a row with few gaps, ask where each missing digit can go.

Why it works

The rule says the digit must appear in the unit. You have shown that it cannot appear in any cell but one. So it appears in that one. You never needed to know what else the cell might have held.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Stopping the sweep at the edge of the neighbouring box. A digit closes its whole row and whole column, right across the grid, not just the next box.
  • Scanning boxes only. Some hidden singles can be found only along a row or a column. When boxes run dry, scan the lines.
  • Placing the digit when two cells are still open. Two open cells are a useful fact (note them), but not yet an answer.

When to look for it

Start every puzzle with hidden singles in boxes, digit by digit from 1 to 9, and go round again after each success, because every placement closes new lines. When a full round gives nothing, look along rows and columns, then turn to naked singles. Only when all three fail do you need pencil marks and the techniques that follow.

Practise it

Play a puzzle at the Easy level, where this technique is all you need, together with its partner single. The first tap on Hint tells you when it is the step to look for.
