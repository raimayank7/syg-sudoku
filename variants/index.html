Sudoku variants: the same idea in other shapes

Once the basic puzzle became popular, setters began to change one thing at a time: the size, the shape of the boxes, the kind of clue. Most variants keep the heart of sudoku, which is "each symbol once in every row, column and region", and add or replace one rule. Here are the ones you are most likely to meet. This site offers the standard 9 by 9 puzzle and the two mini sizes; the others are described so that you know what you are looking at when you find them in a book or newspaper.

Mini sudoku: 4 by 4 and 6 by 6

A 4 by 4 grid uses the digits 1 to 4 and has four boxes of two by two. A 6 by 6 grid uses 1 to 6 and has six boxes, each two rows high and three columns wide. The rule is unchanged. These are the right size for children and for a first lesson, since the whole grid can be held in the mind at once. You can play both sizes here or print them.

Larger grids

A 16 by 16 puzzle has boxes of four by four and uses sixteen symbols, usually the digits with the letters A to G or the numbers 1 to 16. A 25 by 25 puzzle uses boxes of five by five. The logic is the same as in 9 by 9, but scanning takes far longer, so these are tests of stamina more than of cleverness.

Diagonal sudoku (Sudoku X)

The two long diagonals become units as well: each must contain 1 to 9 once. Cells on a diagonal therefore see more cells than usual, which gives extra eliminations in the centre and the corners. Remember that ordinary sudoku has no such rule.

Jigsaw sudoku

Also called irregular or squiggly sudoku. The nine boxes are replaced by nine regions of nine cells with wandering outlines. Rows and columns work as before. Cross-hatching by box no longer applies; you scan region by region and rely more on rows and columns.

Killer sudoku

The grid usually starts with no digits at all. Instead, dotted outlines mark "cages" of neighbouring cells, each with a small number: the sum of the digits in the cage. A digit may not repeat inside a cage. Here arithmetic does matter. Solvers lean on the fact that every row, column and box adds up to 45, and on cages with only one possible combination: a two-cell cage that sums to 3 must be 1 and 2; one that sums to 17 must be 8 and 9.

Samurai sudoku

Five 9 by 9 grids are joined in the shape of an X: one in the middle, and one overlapping each of its corner boxes. Each grid follows the normal rule, and the shared boxes must satisfy both grids at once. You move between grids, using progress in one to unlock another.

Odd and even, greater-than, consecutive

  • Odd-even sudoku shades some cells to show that they hold an odd (or even) digit.
  • Greater-than sudoku puts a sign between neighbouring cells to show which is larger, and often gives no digits at all.
  • Consecutive sudoku marks every border between two cells whose digits differ by exactly one, and, just as importantly, leaves unmarked every border where they do not.

Hyper sudoku (windoku)

Four extra three by three regions are shaded inside the grid, each overlapping four ordinary boxes. They are additional units that must contain 1 to 9. More units mean more constraints, so these puzzles can start with fewer clues.

Letter and picture sudoku

Nine letters, colours or pictures replace the digits, which shows clearly that sudoku was never about numbers. In some letter versions a row or diagonal spells a word when the puzzle is solved. These are a friendly way to introduce the puzzle to someone who says "I am no good at maths".

Do the standard techniques still work?

Yes, with care. Singles, pairs and fish depend only on the idea of units that hold each digit once, so they carry over to every variant; you just have more kinds of unit, or differently shaped ones. Techniques that use the box-and-line overlap, such as pointing pairs, need the regions to overlap lines in the same way, so in jigsaw puzzles they take a different form.

Which to try first

If you solve Hard puzzles in comfort and want a change, Sudoku X is the gentlest step, since only one rule is added. Killer sudoku is the most different and has the largest following of its own. Before any of them, make sure the standard techniques are second nature: every variant assumes them.
