How to solve sudoku faster

Fast solvers are not thinking faster. They waste less: fewer glances that find nothing, fewer notes that are never used, fewer corrections. If your times have stopped improving, the gain is in method. Here is what helps, roughly in order of how much time it saves.

1. Never repair

One mistake costs more time than every other tip on this page saves. Place a digit only when you can state the reason. Solvers who time themselves usually find that their fastest games are the ones that felt unhurried. See common mistakes.

2. Scan in a fixed order

Go through the digits 1 to 9 by cross-hatching, and do not jump about. A fixed order means you never check the same thing twice and never leave a gap unchecked. Start with the digit that has the most clues.

3. Chain your moves

When you place a digit, look first at what that placement has just changed: the same digit in the two neighbouring boxes of the band and of the stack, and the row, column and box that have just lost a gap. The next move is usually within reach of the last one. Finishing a nearly full unit at once, while your eye is there, saves a later visit.

4. Finish digits and units

When eight of a digit are placed, the ninth has exactly one cell: take it immediately. The number pad on our board shows how many of each digit are left, so a "1 left" is a free move. In the same way, close any row, column or box that is down to one or two gaps.

5. Write fewer notes

Full notes in every cell take a minute or more to write and most are never used. In Easy and Medium puzzles write none. In Hard puzzles try corner notes for pairs only: mark a digit when it has exactly two possible cells in a box. Move to full notes only when that fails. (If you use the Fill notes button the writing costs nothing, but reading a full grid of notes is still slower than scanning.)

6. Enter digits efficiently

  • On a computer, use the keyboard: arrow keys to move, digits to place, N to switch notes, Backspace to erase. It is much faster than the mouse.
  • On a phone, hold it so that your thumbs reach the number pad without shifting your grip. Tap the cell with one thumb and the digit with the other.

7. Know the patterns by sight

At Hard and Expert level, time goes into finding the one step that unlocks the grid. The cure is familiarity. Read the worked examples for pointing pairs, naked pairs and the XY-Wing until you can spot each in a few seconds, then use the first tap of the Hint button as a trainer: it tells you which technique is available and where, without showing the move.

8. Practise on purpose

  1. Pick one level and play it daily for a week. The daily puzzle gives everyone the same grid, so you can compare times with a friend.
  2. Do not use hints in timed games. Our board keeps your best time for each level only from games without hints.
  3. After each game, ask where the longest pause was, and what move ended it. Next time, look for that kind of move sooner.
  4. Once a week, play one level higher, slowly. It makes your usual level feel easy.

What times are normal?

We do not publish target times, because we have no honest data to base them on, and because phones, paper and keyboards give very different times for the same puzzle. Your own record is the only fair comparison. It is saved in your browser and shown on the home page under the board.

Speed is optional

Many people play sudoku precisely because it is slow. If timing makes it less pleasant, ignore the clock: tap Pause, or simply do not look. The puzzle is the same either way.
