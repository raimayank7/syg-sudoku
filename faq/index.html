Sudoku questions and answers

Short answers to what players ask most, about the puzzle itself and about how this site works. For the rules, see how to play; for words you do not know, the glossary.

Is sudoku a maths puzzle?

No. The digits are used only as nine different symbols. You never add, subtract or multiply. Any nine symbols would do, and some puzzles use letters or colours instead.

Does every sudoku have only one solution?

Every properly made one does. All puzzles generated on this site are checked by a search program to have exactly one solution before you see them. If a puzzle from another source seems to have two answers, a clue was probably copied wrongly; our solver page can test it.

Do I ever need to guess?

Not on this site. Each puzzle here is also solved, before it is shown, with the step-by-step techniques we teach, so a logical move always exists. Elsewhere, some very hard puzzles need long chains of reasoning that feel close to guessing.

What is the smallest number of clues a sudoku can have?

Seventeen. A computer search published in 2014 showed that no puzzle with 16 clues has a single solution. Our generator does not aim for the minimum; its harder puzzles usually have 24 to 30 clues.

Are the puzzles made by a person or a program?

By a program that runs in your own browser. It fills a grid at random, removes clues in mirror pairs while the solution stays unique, then grades the result by the techniques needed. Nothing is downloaded from a list.

Is the daily puzzle the same for everyone?

Yes. The date and the level set the starting number of the generator, so everybody who opens the daily page on the same date gets the same grid at each level. The date is the one on your own device. You can pick an earlier date to play a puzzle you missed.

Where are my game and my best times saved?

In your browser's own storage on your device. Nothing is sent to us. If you clear your browser data or use another device, the saved game and times are not there.

Can I play without the internet?

After you have opened a page once, it usually opens again without a connection, and the puzzles are made on your device. We do not promise full offline use, because browsers decide how long to keep saved pages.

How does the hint work?

The first tap names the technique that applies and where to look. The second tap explains the step fully and offers to place the digit. A game finished with hints is not counted for your best time.

What does the Check button do?

It compares the digits you have entered with the solution and marks any that differ in red. It does not fill anything in. If you want this to happen automatically, tick "Show mistakes as I type".

Why did a digit turn red by itself?

Because the same digit already stands in that row, column or box, which breaks the rule. The board always shows such clashes. A digit that is wrong without clashing is shown only when you tap Check.

Can I print the puzzles?

Yes. The print page makes one to twelve new puzzles at the level you choose, with one, two, four or six on a page and an optional answer sheet. You can also print the puzzle you are playing with the Print button under the board.

Can I share a puzzle with a friend?

Yes. The address of a puzzle page ends with the level and the puzzle number. Copy the address, or use Share after you finish, and your friend gets the same grid.

Is there a version for children?

Yes. The kids page has 4 by 4 and 6 by 6 grids that use the digits 1 to 4 and 1 to 6, with the same rule and larger numbers.

Does playing sudoku make you cleverer?

We make no health or brain-training claims. Sudoku is a pleasant way to practise careful reasoning and attention. If you enjoy it, that is reason enough to play.

I found a mistake on the site. Whom do I tell?

Please use the contact page and give the address of the page. If it concerns a puzzle, include the puzzle number shown under the board.

Still have a question?

Use the contact page. If it is about solving, the tips, the technique guides and the page on mistakes cover most situations. If it is about a level, see how puzzles are graded.
